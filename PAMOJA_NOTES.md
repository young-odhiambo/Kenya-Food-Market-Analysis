# Note for Pamoja: Maize prices in Kenya, 2006–2019

**Key point:** Maize is cheapest at harvest and dearest mid-year, but holding maize until then is a gamble, not a sure gain. Households in the far north and north-east pay the most for maize.

All figures are from monthly market prices for 2006–2019 (wholesale prices per 90 kg bag in five large markets, and retail prices per kg in nine county markets). Prices are in Kenyan shillings (KES) and are not adjusted for inflation.

## Advice for farmers

**1. Don't sell your whole harvest in January.**
Wholesale maize is cheapest in January (average **KES 2,330** per bag) and dearest in June (**KES 2,839**). That is about **KES 510 more per bag, or 22%**, for maize sold in the lean season.

**2. Store only part of your harvest, and store it well.**
June was higher than January in **73% of market-years** (44 of 60), but the **median gain was only KES 356 per bag**. In 1 year in 4 storing lost money, by up to **KES 781 per bag** (Mombasa, 2010). Sell enough at harvest to cover urgent needs. Store the rest in hermetic bags, because if storage losses and costs come to more than about KES 350 per bag, holding does not pay.

**3. Don't expect a high-price year to repeat.**
2017 had the highest average price (**KES 3,744** per bag). In 2018 the average fell by **KES 1,335**, the largest drop in the data. After a year of very high prices, plan for lower prices and don't hold maize too long.

## Advice for county officers

**4. Focus food-price support on Turkana and Mandera.**
From 2015 to 2019, **Lodwar** (Turkana) and **Mandera** were among the three most expensive retail maize markets **in every single year**. Households in Lodwar paid an average of **KES 75.85 per kg**, about **twice** the **KES 36.19** paid in Kitui, the cheapest market. Across all retail markets, households pay about **68% more per kg** than wholesale prices (KES 47.92 vs KES 28.54). Better roads and transport, market links with surplus areas such as Eldoret, and food assistance in the lean season would do the most good in these two counties.

## Limits to keep in mind

These are market prices, not farm-gate prices, and they are not adjusted for inflation. The storage figures leave out pests, aflatoxin, weight loss, bag costs and the cost of waiting for cash. Retail and wholesale prices come from different markets, so the 68% gap includes transport costs to remote areas as well as trader margins.
